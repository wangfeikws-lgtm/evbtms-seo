The question that opens every serious HVCH conversation:

"You sell A, Q and H series heaters. Which one does my project need?"

Same answer every time: start with required kW, then the voltage window. The letters mark power bands and voltage reach, not good-better-best.

One, power. H covers 5–14 kW, A covers 6–18 kW, Q covers 15–35 kW. The bands overlap — that's exactly where buyers get stuck. When two series both cover your kW, power has done its job. Move on to the voltage window.

Two, voltage. Rated voltage is customizable on all three series, so stop there and look at the operating window: A runs 400–800V, H reaches 400–1000V, Q spans 400–1100V. A 12 kW heater on a 900V platform is an H conversation, not an A one. Send min/nominal/max — the nominal number alone misleads.

Three, same DNA. All three run -40 to +85°C, carry IP67, speak CAN plus an enable signal, and self-diagnose faults. So the series letter never tells you about quality tier. It tells you power × voltage.

Scoping a heater? Send vehicle type, required kW, min/nominal/max HV, 12/24V supply, coolant and flow, inlet/target temp, ambient range, installation envelope, and CAN requirements. Enough for a fit assessment.
