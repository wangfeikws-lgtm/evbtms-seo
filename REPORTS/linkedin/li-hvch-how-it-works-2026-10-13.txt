Most engineers size a coolant heater by two numbers: volts and kilowatts. Then the prototype arrives and the vehicle controller cannot talk to it.

A coolant heater is not a resistor in a box. It is a five-stage system. Power in: 400, 600 or 800 V, where the nominal number is only the start and the operating range matters more. The PTC element turns power into heat and self-regulates with temperature. A pump circulates coolant through the heater; the heater can sit anywhere since coolant does the moving. Valves and pumps route warmed coolant to cabin, battery and defrost. And CAN: the controller sends enable, target power, mode and temperature target, while the heater reports back status, coolant temperature, faults and diagnostics.

Right voltage and power with the wrong protocol is still a project delay. Settle the protocol before prototype validation, not during.

Sizing a heater? Bring the full picture: vehicle type, ambient conditions, flow rate, actual voltage range, installation space. Power alone never told the whole story.
