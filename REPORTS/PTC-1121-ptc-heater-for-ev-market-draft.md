# PTC Heater for EV Market: What Buyers Should Know Before They Order

The EV PTC heater market is growing fast. Every report says so. But if you read three reports, you get three different market sizes. That is not a mistake. Each firm counts a different box. Before you order a heater, you need to know what box you are buying into.

Here is the short version. High voltage coolant heaters are now the mainstream product for electric vehicles. Cabin heating takes the biggest slice of demand. Battery heating is the second job. Asia makes most of the heaters. And the market is growing fast enough that new suppliers keep showing up. Some of them are good. Some are not. The market numbers will not tell you which is which. Your specification will.

## The numbers disagree. Here is why

One research firm puts the PTC coolant heater market for EVs at $521 million in 2025, growing to $1.27 billion by 2032. That is a 13.5% annual growth rate. A second firm counts high voltage PTC water heaters only and gets $1.2 billion in 2025, growing to $3.5 billion by 2035. A third counts every PTC heater for EVs and gets $1.3 billion in 2024, growing to $5 billion by 2035.

Three numbers, three scopes. The first counts coolant heaters only. The second counts high voltage water heaters only. The third counts the whole PTC heater category, air heaters included. The growth rates agree more than the sizes do. All three land around 11 to 13% a year. The market is growing. The exact size depends on what you count.

Do not buy on a headline number. A billion-dollar market does not make a bad heater good. It just means more sellers will knock on your door. Read the growth rate, not the size. Then ask the supplier the questions that actually matter. Our [selection guide](https://evbtms.com/high-voltage-ptc-heater-selection-guide/) lists them.

## Where the demand actually sits

Cabin heating is the biggest job. One report puts it at about 60% of the coolant heater market. That makes sense. Every electric vehicle needs a warm cabin in winter. There is no engine waste heat to steal. The heater has to do the whole job.

Battery heating is the second job. A cold battery charges slow and gives back less. Preheating fixes that before the vehicle leaves. Buses preheat at the depot. Trucks preheat before a cold start. Cars preheat on the charger. The same coolant heater often does both jobs. Our [automotive PTC guide](https://evbtms.com/ptc-heater-automotive/) covers where one heater sits when it feeds cabin and battery together.

High voltage takes the biggest share of the product mix. One report puts high voltage at about 70% of the market in 2025. Low voltage heaters still sell for small vehicles and auxiliary jobs. But the volume sits with high voltage. If you build electric buses or trucks, you are buying high voltage. That is where the suppliers are. That is also where the quality spread is widest.

By vehicle, the demand splits three ways. Cars buy the most units. Buses and trucks buy fewer units but bigger heaters. A bus heater runs 15 to 35 kW. A car heater runs much smaller. The bus and truck end is where a buyer can get hurt. The numbers are bigger, the duty is harder, and a wrong heater costs a season. Our [electric bus guide](https://evbtms.com/high-voltage-coolant-heater-electric-bus/) covers what changes when the vehicle gets big.

## High voltage is the direction

The market is moving toward higher voltage platforms. One report notes integrated high voltage coolant heaters in the 300 to 800 V range, built to heat cabin and battery together in a more compact package. That matches what we see on our own line. Our [A series](https://evbtms.com/products/high-voltage-coolant-heater/) runs 400 to 800 V. Our Q series runs up to 1100 V. If your platform window runs past 800 V, say so up front. Do not find out at integration.

China tightened the rules on coolant in October 2025. The GB 29743.2-2025 standard caps coolant electrical conductivity at 100 μS/cm. The point is to cut the risk of battery short circuits. That rule pushes the whole supply chain toward better coolant control. It also means a heater supplier who cannot talk about coolant quality is behind the market. Ask about it. A good supplier has an answer.

Heat pumps get a lot of press. They help on mild days. Below about -10°C they struggle, and the PTC heater carries the load. That is why a growing market still runs on PTC. The heat pump is a helper. The PTC heater is the guarantee. Read our [guide to what a PTC heater is](https://evbtms.com/what-is-a-ptc-heater/) if the basics are new. The PTC element backs off as it warms up. It cannot run away and cook itself. That self-limiting behavior is the reason the market trusts it.

## What this means when you buy

A growing market means more choice. It also means more noise. New sellers enter every year. Some bring a real product. Some bring a catalog page and a price. The market reports will not sort them for you. They count revenue. They do not test heaters.

Judge a supplier the way you would judge any other vehicle part. Ask for the rated conditions behind the kilowatt number: voltage, coolant, flow rate, inlet temperature. A kilowatt without conditions is not a real number. Our [sizing guide](https://evbtms.com/high-voltage-coolant-heater-sizing-evs/) explains the inputs. If a supplier cannot give them, the number is not real.

Ask about control. Our A and Q series run on CAN plus an enable signal, coordinated by the vehicle controller. The E series uses a thermostat and has no CAN, for simpler thermal control. Check what your vehicle speaks before you order. A CAN heater on a vehicle with no CAN bus cannot be controlled. It arrives, it sits in the warehouse.

Ask about volume and lead time. We have built high voltage coolant heaters since 2021. Our A series covers 6 to 18 kW. Our Q series covers 15 to 35 kW. Our E series covers 20 to 24 kW. Big platforms stack units. Six 35 kW units on one truck give 210 kW of heating where a single heater cannot reach. If your vehicle sits outside the A, Q and E boxes, ask us about the H series. Specifications are provided per project. It exists for exactly that case.

And ask who else buys from them. Our [manufacturer guide](https://evbtms.com/ptc-heater-manufacturer/) covers how to judge a supplier on more than price. A supplier who has shipped to bus and truck platforms has already survived the hard duty. A supplier who only quotes car heaters has not.

## What to ask before you order

Do not start with "how much." A price without numbers is a guess. We do not guess. Send us the numbers below, and we come back with a series, a price, and a sample plan.

- Vehicle type and use: car, bus, truck, or off-road. And where the vehicle sleeps at night.
- Cabin heating, battery heating, or both. And which one matters more on a cold morning.
- Platform voltage: minimum, nominal, maximum. Not just nominal.
- Power need at your coldest operating temperature. Not at room temperature.
- Coolant: what mixture, and what flow rate.
- Control: CAN plus enable, or thermostat.
- Annual volume.

[Start with the numbers](https://evbtms.com/high-voltage-coolant-heater-selection-quote/). The market will keep growing. The reports will keep disagreeing on the size. None of that changes what you need to do. Specify the heater for your vehicle. ## FAQ

**How big is the EV PTC heater market?**

It depends on what you count. Research firms put the 2025 market at roughly $0.5 to $1.3 billion, growing to $1.3 to $5 billion by the early 2030s. The growth rate is the steadier number: about 11 to 13% a year across the three reports. The market is growing. The exact size depends on whether the firm counts coolant heaters only, high voltage water heaters only, or every PTC heater for EVs.

**What kind of PTC heater sells most for EVs?**

High voltage coolant heaters. One report puts high voltage at about 70% of the coolant heater market in 2025. Cabin heating is the biggest application, at about 60%. Battery heating is the second job, and one heater often does both. Our A series covers 6 to 18 kW and our Q series covers 15 to 35 kW, both for high voltage platforms.

**Do I still need a PTC heater if my EV has a heat pump?**

Yes. A heat pump helps on mild days. Below about -10°C it struggles, and the PTC heater carries the load. That is why a growing market still runs on PTC. The heat pump is a helper. The PTC heater is the guarantee. Read our [guide to what a PTC heater is](https://evbtms.com/what-is-a-ptc-heater/) for the basics.

**Which region makes most EV PTC heaters?**

Asia Pacific leads. One report puts it at more than half of global revenue, driven by large-scale EV production in China. Europe follows on winter performance needs and stricter emissions rules. North America is growing too. Where a heater is made matters less than whether the supplier can meet your voltage window, your coolant rules, and your duty cycle.

**What should I check before ordering from a new supplier?**

Ask for the rated conditions behind the kilowatt number: voltage, coolant, flow rate, inlet temperature. Check the control interface against your vehicle: CAN plus enable, or thermostat. Ask about volume, lead time, and who else buys from them. Our [manufacturer guide](https://evbtms.com/ptc-heater-manufacturer/) covers how to judge a supplier on more than price, and our [selection guide](https://evbtms.com/high-voltage-ptc-heater-selection-guide/) lists the numbers to send with an enquiry.
