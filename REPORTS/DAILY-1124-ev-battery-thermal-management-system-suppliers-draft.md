# EV Battery Thermal Management System Suppliers: How to Shortlist One for Your Electric Bus

Short answer first. A good shortlist has three names, not ten. Pick suppliers who build complete systems for buses, not just parts. Check that their cooling and heating numbers fit your route, not a lab sheet. And watch how they answer. A supplier who asks for your bus model, your battery, your climate and your charging pattern before quoting is worth talking to. One who sends a brochure and a price is not.

You will meet the same names on every list. Modine. Webasto. Newbase. TKT. Brogen. Grayson. We are EVLINK, and we build BTMS for commercial EVs too. This is how we think a buyer should judge us, and them.

## Start with the bus, not the catalogue

Every BTMS conversation that goes wrong starts the same way. Someone asks for a price on a 5 kW unit before anyone has said what bus it goes on.

A city bus that fast-charges at the depot at noon in July needs a different system from a coach that slow-charges overnight in a cold depot. Same battery, different day. The cooling load, the heating load, the duty cycle, all of it changes. I have seen buyers order the right kW number for the wrong day and then blame the hardware.

So start with four facts. What bus. What battery. Where it runs. How it charges. Any supplier who takes your order without those four facts is guessing. You are the one who pays for the guess.

## What a complete BTMS actually includes

A battery thermal management system is not a cold plate. It is a loop, and every part of the loop has to work with the rest.

The refrigerant side moves heat out of the pack. The coolant side carries it to the cold plate. The PTC heater puts heat back in when it is cold. The controller talks to the battery management system and the vehicle controller over CAN. Sensors watch temperature, pressure and flow. If one part is wrong, the whole loop pays for it.

Our range runs on that logic. The LDC300HA-3KW cools at 3 kW and takes DC 400 to 750 V. It weighs 24 kg. The LDC502HA-5KW cools at 5 kW, single cooling, DC 400 to 860 V. The LDC560HA-5KW cools at 5 kW and heats with a 6 kW PTC as standard. The LDC280HA-12KW cools at 12 kW and heats with an 8 kW PTC. All four share the same backbone. CAN 2.0 at 250 kbps. R134a refrigerant. 50% glycol coolant. 3.5 Bar. COP above 2.0. IP67. Working range minus 35 to 60 degrees. Three year warranty, ten year design life. Noise under 75 dB.

Those numbers are the floor, not the sales pitch. If a supplier cannot give you the same numbers for their unit, in writing, that tells you something too.

## The names you will meet

Webasto and Eberspächer show up on every PTC heater list. MAHLE, Valeo, Hanon Systems and Denso cover the big Tier 1 ground. Big names are safe. That is also their weakness. A big supplier can take six weeks to answer a technical question. A smaller one can answer in a day and build to your drawing. Size tells you nothing about fit. Ask who answers, and how fast, and whether the answer comes from an engineer or from sales.

## Ask these five questions before you shortlist

- What cooling capacity do you guarantee at my worst ambient temperature, not at 25 degrees in a lab?
- How does the system heat the pack in winter, and does it precondition before fast charging?
- Does the controller speak CAN to my BMS and VCU, and what happens when the bus loses comms?
- What validation has the unit passed — vibration, water, live performance — and can I see the test record?
- What is the MOQ, the lead time, and the sample process? And who do I call when the first unit has a problem?

Five questions. Any supplier who answers all five in writing belongs on your shortlist. Any supplier who answers two of them does not. The point is not to be difficult. The point is that a bus program runs for ten years, and the supplier you pick now is the one you call in year seven.

## FAQ

**Who supplies battery thermal management systems for electric buses?**
Modine, Webasto, Newbase, TKT, Brogen, Grayson, MAHLE, Valeo and Hanon Systems all supply BTMS or BTMS components for buses. EVLINK supplies complete BTMS units for commercial EVs in 3, 5 and 12 kW. Shortlist three, not ten, and judge them on the five questions above.

**What cooling capacity does an electric bus BTMS need?**
It depends on the pack and the route. Our range covers 3 kW for small packs, 5 kW for city buses and 12 kW for large packs and fast-charge duty. The right size comes from your battery capacity, your worst ambient temperature and your charging pattern, not from a catalogue table.

**Does a BTMS also heat the battery?**
Yes, it should. Our 5 kW cooling-and-heating unit carries a 6 kW PTC heater as standard, and the 12 kW unit carries an 8 kW PTC heater. Cold packs charge slowly and age fast. Heating is not an option. It is part of the system.

**How does a BTMS talk to the bus?**
Over CAN 2.0 at 250 kbps, coordinated with the battery management system and the vehicle controller. Ask any supplier what happens when communication drops. The answer matters more than the protocol name.

**What should a BTMS quotation include?**
Cooling and heating capacity at your ambient temperature, voltage range, weight and dimensions, CAN integration, validation test records, warranty, MOQ, lead time and the sample process. If a quotation has a price but no test record, it is not a quotation. It is a guess.

**How long does a BTMS last?**
Our units carry a three year warranty and a ten year design life. Ask every supplier the same two numbers, and ask what wears out first. The honest ones will tell you.
